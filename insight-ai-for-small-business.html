Where to Start With AI in a Small Business

A practical, step-by-step guide for small businesses that want to use artificial intelligence without wasting money.

By Sunrose Technologies5 November 20265 min read

Artificial intelligence is no longer only for large corporations. Today, a small business in Lagos, Leeds or Los Angeles can use the same kinds of tools that once required a research team. The challenge is not access. It is knowing where to start, and avoiding expensive projects that never pay back.

Here is the approach we recommend to every business that asks us about AI.

1. Start with a problem, not with the technology

The question is never "How can we use AI?" It is "Which task costs us the most time, money or customers right now?" Good first candidates are tasks that are repetitive, text-heavy and rule-based: answering common customer questions, sorting enquiries, summarising documents, or preparing first drafts of routine messages.

2. Measure the task before you change it

Write down how long the task takes today, how often it happens and how often mistakes occur. Without this baseline, you cannot tell whether an AI solution is actually helping.

3. Keep a person in the loop

The most reliable early AI projects assist people rather than replace them. An assistant drafts the reply and a staff member approves it. A system flags unusual transactions and a manager reviews them. This keeps quality high while your team builds trust in the tool.

The best first AI project is small, measurable and easy to switch off.

4. Protect your data

Before you send customer or business information to any AI service, check where it is stored, who can access it and whether it is used to train other models. Only share what the task genuinely needs, and follow the data protection laws that apply to you and your customers.

5. Build a small pilot, then expand

Run the new process alongside the old one for a few weeks. Compare time saved, quality and customer feedback against your baseline. If the results are good, expand. If not, you have learned cheaply.

Common first projects that work

  • A website or WhatsApp assistant that answers frequently asked questions and hands complex cases to staff.
  • Automatic sorting and summarising of incoming emails and enquiries.
  • Drafting quotes, proposals and follow-up messages from a short brief.
  • Extracting key details from invoices, receipts and forms.
  • Simple dashboards that highlight unusual changes in sales or stock.
